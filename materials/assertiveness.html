Assertiveness

Standing up for yourself, without stepping on others. Being assertive means saying what you think and need, clearly and respectfully, even when it feels easier to stay quiet or to react strongly. This activity walks through four steps, shows what assertiveness can look like in practice, then lets you try it yourself with six everyday situations.

The four steps

These are the steps behind an assertive response. Tap "+" on any step for a bit more detail.

1

Step 1: Notice how you feel

Name what you are feeling in the moment: annoyed, nervous, embarrassed, hurt. Naming a feeling makes it easier to manage.

There is no wrong feeling to have. The goal is not to switch it off, it is to notice it before you react.

2

Step 2: Notice your instinct

Most people lean one of two ways under pressure: staying silent, or reacting strongly. Both are understandable, but neither usually gets your needs met.

A passive response protects the moment but leaves your own needs unheard. An aggressive response gets your point across but can damage the relationship. Assertive sits between the two.

3

Step 3: Choose your words

Try starting with "I feel", "I'd like" or "I prefer" rather than staying silent or placing blame. It keeps the focus on what you need.

For example: "I feel frustrated when plans change last minute. I'd like a bit more notice next time."

4

Step 4: Act it out, then review

Say what you planned to say. Afterwards, think about how it went and what you might adjust next time.

It will not always go perfectly, and that is fine. Each attempt is useful information for the next one. Assertiveness gets easier with practice.

Think it through

A couple of questions to sit with, on your own or with a partner.

  • Think of a real situation where you stayed quiet, even though you wanted to say something. What could an assertive version sound like?
  • Which is easiest for you to use, passive, aggressive or assertive? Why do you think that is?
