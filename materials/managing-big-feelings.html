Managing Big Feelings

A feeling does not come out of nowhere. This activity looks at how a situation, a thought, a feeling and an action are all connected, how to notice how big a feeling is, and how to choose your response even when a feeling is strong.

How feelings work

A situation leads to a thought. That thought leads to a feeling. That feeling leads to an action. The useful part is this: if the thought changes, the whole cycle can change too.

Situation: You send a friend a message and it takes them a few hours to reply.

Rate the size

Before you can manage a feeling, it helps to notice how big it actually is right now.

  • 10: the most I have ever felt this way
  • 8: this feeling is very big
  • 5: this feeling is medium
  • 3: this feeling is pretty small
  • 1: the least I have ever felt this way
5

That is a medium feeling.

Choosing your response: acting opposite

When a feeling is big, it comes with an urge, a pull towards a certain kind of action. One option is to act opposite to that urge instead. It will not always feel natural, but it can help the feeling come down and stop things getting worse. This idea comes from Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT).

Think a little more

A question to sit with, on your own or with a partner.

Think of a recent moment when a strong feeling almost made you do something big. What might acting opposite have looked like in that moment?

If a feeling ever feels like more than you can handle on your own, it is okay to talk to a trusted adult: a teacher, a school counsellor, or someone in your family you trust.

This activity draws on ideas from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) and the opposite action skill from Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT), an approach developed by psychologist Marsha Linehan.
